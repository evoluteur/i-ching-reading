About the I Ching

The I Ching, or Book of Changes, is a Chinese text built on 64 figures called hexagrams, each with a short passage of commentary and advice. It is one of the oldest books that people still consult.

Lines, trigrams and hexagrams

Line
Either solid (yang) or broken (yin).
Trigram
Three lines. There are eight: Heaven, Earth, Thunder, Water, Mountain, Wind, Fire and Lake.
Hexagram
Two trigrams stacked, six lines in all. Two kinds of line in six places gives 26 = 64 hexagrams.
Changing line
A line at its extreme, about to turn into its opposite. Changing lines turn the first hexagram into a second one, which shows where things are heading.

Casting a hexagram

The common modern method throws three coins six times, building the hexagram from the bottom line up. Each throw adds up to 6, 7, 8 or 9 (heads count 3 and tails 2): 6 is an old yin line and 9 an old yang line, and both are changing lines, while 7 is a steady yang line and 8 a steady yin line. The older yarrow-stalk method gives the same four results with different odds.

A short history

  • Late Western ZhouThe core text, the Zhouyi, takes shape around the 9th century BCE as a manual of divination: the hexagrams, a statement for each, and a saying for each line.
  • Warring States to HanA set of commentaries called the Ten Wings is added, and the book becomes one of the Confucian classics. Tradition credits Confucius with the commentaries; modern scholars see many hands.
  • 1703Gottfried Leibniz, sent a diagram of the hexagrams by the Jesuit Joachim Bouvet, sees in them his own binary numbers.
  • 1923 and 1950Richard Wilhelm publishes his German translation, and Cary F. Baynes’s English version follows in 1950 with a foreword by Carl Jung.

This app follows the traditional King Wen sequence, the order in which the hexagrams appear in the book.

How to read it

Traditionally you bring a real question, cast a hexagram, and read its text, then the text of any changing lines, then the second hexagram. Like tarot, it works best as a prompt for thinking a situation through rather than as a prediction. The summaries and advice in this app were written for it, so they are short pointers and not a replacement for a full translation.
