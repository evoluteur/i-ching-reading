Difficulty at the Beginning (屯, Zhūn) is hexagram 3 of the 64 hexagrams of the I Ching, in the traditional King Wen order. It is made of Water (☵) above Thunder (☳).

Meaning of hexagram 3

Something new is straining to grow, and the start is messy and uncertain. Confusion at this stage is normal, not a sign of failure.

Advice

Do not rush. Build a little structure, ask for help, and take one small step at a time.

The two trigrams

Difficulty at the Beginning places ☵ Water (Kǎn), deep, flowing and dangerous, above ☳ Thunder (Zhèn), arousing, sudden and energetic. The lower trigram is the inner side of the situation and the upper one its outer side.

The six lines of Difficulty at the Beginning

A hexagram is read from the bottom line up. In a reading, a changing line (a 6 or a 9 in the three-coin method) draws attention to its position.

  1. First line (yang, solid): the beginning. The root of the matter: early stirrings, foundations, and what has not yet shown itself.
  2. Second line (yin, broken): the inner circle. Your immediate surroundings and steady supports: how things work at close range.
  3. Third line (yin, broken): the threshold. A point of tension between inner and outer, often a test or a restless push forward.
  4. Fourth line (yin, broken): the approach. Stepping into wider circles or nearer to authority; caution and good relationships matter here.
  5. Fifth line (yang, solid): the center of power. The place of influence and clear leadership: where the situation is most fully expressed.
  6. Sixth line (yin, broken): the culmination. The far edge of the situation: completion, overreach, or letting go.
