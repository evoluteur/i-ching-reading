Hexagram 41: Decrease

Decrease (損, Sǔn) is hexagram 41 of the 64 hexagrams of the I Ching, in the traditional King Wen order. It is made of Mountain (☶) above Lake (☱).

Meaning of hexagram 41

Reducing the outer to strengthen the inner. A modest, sincere offering carries more weight than a lavish one.

Advice

Cut back on the unnecessary and give sincerely, not extravagantly.

The two trigrams

Decrease places ☶ Mountain (Gèn), still, steady and restrained, above ☱ Lake (Duì), joyful, open and pleasing. The lower trigram is the inner side of the situation and the upper one its outer side.

The six lines of Decrease

A hexagram is read from the bottom line up. In a reading, a changing line (a 6 or a 9 in the three-coin method) draws attention to its position.

  1. First line (yang, solid): the beginning. The root of the matter: early stirrings, foundations, and what has not yet shown itself.
  2. Second line (yang, solid): the inner circle. Your immediate surroundings and steady supports: how things work at close range.
  3. Third line (yin, broken): the threshold. A point of tension between inner and outer, often a test or a restless push forward.
  4. Fourth line (yin, broken): the approach. Stepping into wider circles or nearer to authority; caution and good relationships matter here.
  5. Fifth line (yin, broken): the center of power. The place of influence and clear leadership: where the situation is most fully expressed.
  6. Sixth line (yang, solid): the culmination. The far edge of the situation: completion, overreach, or letting go.
