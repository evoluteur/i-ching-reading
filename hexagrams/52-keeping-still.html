Keeping Still (艮, Gèn) is hexagram 52 of the 64 hexagrams of the I Ching, in the traditional King Wen order. It is made of Mountain (☶) above Mountain (☶).

Meaning of hexagram 52

A time to stop, like a mountain. Quieting the mind and holding back from restless action gives clarity.

Advice

Pause, find your stillness and act only when you know it is the right moment.

The two trigrams

Keeping Still doubles the trigram ☶ Mountain (Gèn): still, steady and restrained above and below, the quality of mountain at its purest.

The six lines of Keeping Still

A hexagram is read from the bottom line up. In a reading, a changing line (a 6 or a 9 in the three-coin method) draws attention to its position.

  1. First line (yin, broken): the beginning. The root of the matter: early stirrings, foundations, and what has not yet shown itself.
  2. Second line (yin, broken): the inner circle. Your immediate surroundings and steady supports: how things work at close range.
  3. Third line (yang, solid): the threshold. A point of tension between inner and outer, often a test or a restless push forward.
  4. Fourth line (yin, broken): the approach. Stepping into wider circles or nearer to authority; caution and good relationships matter here.
  5. Fifth line (yin, broken): the center of power. The place of influence and clear leadership: where the situation is most fully expressed.
  6. Sixth line (yang, solid): the culmination. The far edge of the situation: completion, overreach, or letting go.
